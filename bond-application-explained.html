01 / 08

What this transaction is trying to achieve

The documents describe a proposal for Lebogang Antonio Ribeiro de Souza, referred to as Antonio in the emails, to join Denise in buying a property for one million nine hundred thousand rand and applying for a joint home loan. The reason given is that Denise cannot qualify for the required loan on her own. Antonio’s proposed involvement is therefore as a co-purchaser of the property and a co-borrower under the loan, with potentially substantial personal financial responsibility. The central concern is that the offer contains conflicting dates and incomplete signing details, while Arthur’s statement that Antonio becomes legally committed only on registration is unsafe to rely on.

This explanation is based on all seven pages of the supplied email record and all twenty-five pages of the supplied offer to purchase. References to pages mean the pages in the electronic document, because the printed numbering does not always match. The emails cover the seventeenth to the nineteenth of September, two thousand and twenty-six. They establish the discussions that had taken place by then, but they do not prove what may have happened after the last message. Instructions inside the correspondence, such as requests to print, sign or supply documents, describe what the participants asked Antonio to do. They are not instructions to carry out those actions as part of this review.

For someone coming from a frontend development background, the useful distinction is between three separate arrangements that have to fit together. The offer to purchase is the agreement between the buyers and sellers about buying the property. The transfer process changes the registered ownership of that property. The home loan agreement is the separate arrangement under which a bank lends money and the borrowers promise to repay it. A mortgage bond gives the bank security over the property. Being listed on one document does not, by itself, answer every question about rights and responsibilities under the others. This is a property transaction rather than a software codebase, so the plan of action here concerns the documents, decisions and commitments needed to make the transaction clear.

The people and organisations involved

Antonio is the proposed joint purchaser and bond co-applicant. He supplies bank statements and asks questions about ownership, liability, costs, the percentages allocated to the applicants and the point at which he would become committed. The documents use variations of his name, but the co-applicant disclosure identifies him as Lebogang Antonio Ribeiro de Souza. His correct full name should be used consistently in the final documents.

Denise Alice Ribeiro De-Souza-Jacobs is the original purchaser and the intended joint borrower. The emails indicate that she occupies the property and needs the purchase to proceed if she is to retain that accommodation. Lolly Ribeiro De Souza, addressed by Antonio as Aunty, forwards his bank statements to Jennifer. Lolly appears to be Denise rather than a separate third applicant, because Denise’s purchaser information records the same email address used by Lolly. This is an inference from the documents, and the identity should be confirmed directly. The supplied material does not establish a separate third borrower.

Jennifer Wagener is the Harcourts Blue estate agent coordinating the offer, signatures, supporting documents and communication between the seller and the bond originator. Johan Wagener is her colleague in Team Waggie. He appears in the offer as the listing agent and signs the agent documentation. Their role is to facilitate the property transaction.

Arthur Nicholson is a Property Finance Specialist at ooba. He is arranging the application to banks and gathering the information they will need. His role is that of a bond originator’s representative. He is not the lender deciding whether to advance the money, and he is not the conveyancing attorney responsible for the legal transfer of the property.

The seller side names Leon Le Roux and the estate of the late Dorathea Louiza Le Roux. The deceased’s name is handwritten in the offer, so its spelling should be checked against the formal estate documentation. Johan Liebenberg of VDT Attorneys is identified as executor on the seller side and is also named under conveyancing attorney in the seller’s information form. An executor administers a deceased person’s estate, while a conveyancing attorney handles the legal work involved in transferring property. His exact appointment and authority in this transaction should be confirmed. Johan Liebenberg and Johan Wagener are different people with different roles.

The remaining parties are the prospective banks. They would assess the applicants’ finances and decide whether to lend, how much to lend, the interest rate, the repayment term and any additional conditions. No actual bank approval or home loan agreement is included in the supplied material. There is therefore no confirmed lender or final set of lending terms to assess.

What the email conversation establishes

The sequence begins when Antonio sends bank statements to his aunt. Those statements reach Jennifer and Arthur. Arthur says that he has prepared most of the application information, but still needs the signed offer to purchase, Denise’s latest three months of bank statements and a telephone interview with Antonio. Antonio provides his telephone number and asks to see the documentation and understand exactly what commitment is proposed.

Arthur confirms that the intended application is in the joint names of Antonio and Denise. When Antonio asks about percentages among three applicants, Arthur queries where the third applicant comes into the arrangement. Arthur says that, because of Denise’s age, they can try for a twenty-year repayment term. He also says they can try to include transfer and registration costs in the loan. Both statements describe possibilities to be requested from a bank. Neither is a confirmed loan term or a promise that the costs will be financed.

Jennifer asks Antonio to print and sign the offer in ink, attend to the blue dots marking the places for initials, signatures or information, enter his current residential address and supply proof of that address. She says a version with all signatures will then be forwarded for the application process. The urgency comes from the seller’s stated requirement for bond approval before the thirtieth of September, two thousand and twenty-six. Jennifer says Denise would otherwise need to move and refers to the rental agreement expiring on that date. This records the position being communicated by the seller and agent. It does not, by itself, establish a lawful eviction date or prove that all legal requirements for eviction have been met.

Antonio’s last message says that he will discuss everything with his aunt and go through the documents. The correspondence supplied does not show a completed bank approval or his signed acceptance. Sending financial information and asking questions should therefore not be confused with proof that all the proposed contracts have been completed.

The offer concerns the freehold property at ninety-nine Wilson Street, Witfield, Boksburg, described as Erf ninety, Portion five. Freehold means the transaction concerns ownership of the registered property itself, rather than a sectional title unit in a scheme. The title conditions and any relevant servitudes still matter. A servitude is a legally recognised right affecting land, such as a right of access or a right connected with services. The document lists both Denise and Antonio as purchasers and appears to be an earlier February agreement that was subsequently adapted to add Antonio in September.

The purchase price is one million nine hundred thousand rand. The deposit is recorded as none. The proposed bond is also one million nine hundred thousand rand, so the written financing request covers the purchase price in full. It does not expressly include additional transaction costs. A zero deposit does not mean there will be no money to pay upfront, because transfer costs and other expenses can still become payable separately. The price is payable to the seller when transfer takes place. Clause one point two point one requires a guarantee within thirty days of the bond being granted. In this context, a guarantee is an undertaking used to secure payment to the seller when the specified transfer conditions are met, rather than an immediate cash payment of the whole price.

Occupation is written as the first of March, two thousand and twenty-six. That is a historical date that needs clarification before Antonio joins the arrangement. The offer records occupational rent of seventeen thousand rand per month for the period when occupation and registered ownership do not coincide. Occupational rent is payment for using the property while someone else is still the registered owner. It is not a quoted monthly home loan repayment. Handwritten wording mentions water usage and excludes rates and taxes, but the precise allocation of utilities and other charges needs to be clarified.

The purchaser must pay the transfer and bond registration costs when the conveyancer demands them. These include transfer duty if applicable. Transfer duty is a tax associated with a qualifying property acquisition and is separate from the attorney’s professional charges. The offer does not allocate those costs between Antonio and Denise. It also does not record the percentage of the property that each would own.

05 / 08

The property’s condition and included items

The property is sold voetstoets. Broadly, that means it is offered in its existing condition, subject to the contract and applicable law. The word should not be read as a guarantee that everything works, or as a complete explanation of every legal remedy that might exist. The offer includes permanent fixtures and fittings, and the disclosure specifically says that the bird cages will remain on the property.

The condition disclosure identifies old carpets, potentially cracked tiles, cracked or broken windows, a stove on which only the two rear plates work, and a faulty top oven. It says that the seller will not repair or replace these items. The buyers should therefore account for possible repair and replacement expenses rather than assume those costs will be absorbed by the seller. Building plans are said to be available for handover on registration. The general fixtures clause promises working items, which creates a point that needs to be reconciled expressly with the specific list of defective items. The final wording should make clear which items are excluded from that general promise.

Source: Offer, pages 9–10 and 21–23.

06 / 08

What Antonio would be taking on

Antonio’s first proposed commitment is to become a party to the purchase contract. Page one names him as a purchaser, page fourteen identifies him as a joint purchaser, and page twenty-four records his involvement in both the purchase and financing. The added co-applicant schedule does not limit his responsibility to a small contribution or a temporary period. Calling someone a co-applicant describes their participation in the application process; it does not, by itself, create a cap on what they may owe under the contracts they sign.

His second potential commitment is responsibility for the whole outstanding home loan. Arthur expressly answers yes when Antonio asks whether he could be responsible for the full repayment or outstanding balance if Denise cannot pay. Joint home loan terms commonly provide for joint and several liability. In plain language, that can allow the lender to pursue one borrower for the full amount owed under the agreement, rather than being limited to that borrower’s privately agreed share. Absa’s published explanation says that even when borrowers manage separate portions of a joint home loan, they can remain jointly and severally liable for the total loan. This explains a common lending structure, but it is not evidence that Absa is the lender here. Antonio’s actual exposure must be confirmed in the bank agreement eventually offered.

For example, Antonio and Denise might privately agree that Antonio will contribute twenty percent of the repayments. That arrangement would not, by itself, limit the bank to claiming twenty percent from him. The family’s allocation of payments and the bank’s contractual rights are different matters. Any limitation on the bank’s rights would have to be accepted by the bank in the relevant agreement.

His ownership entitlement is a separate issue again. Arthur says the property will be in joint names, but the offer does not specify ownership percentages, initial contributions, the distribution of proceeds if the property is sold, or how one owner could buy out the other. The material therefore provides no basis for assuming a fifty-fifty split or a three-way split. The fact that a person may be liable for a large debt does not, without more information, establish the precise ownership share that person will receive.

Antonio also needs to understand the expenses beyond the price. Clause eight places transfer and bond registration costs on the purchaser. Arthur says only that they can try to finance those costs. If the bank does not agree, the purchasers must fund them themselves. The offer does not state how Antonio and Denise will divide those expenses. The occupation and transfer clauses also deal with property expenses and the transfer of ownership risk. Those provisions need to be understood alongside the historical occupation date, especially before Antonio accepts responsibility for charges that may relate to a period before he became involved.

The offer also requires cooperation with obtaining finance. Clause two point one point six authorises the agency and originator to apply for a mortgage bond. Required supporting documents must be supplied within five working days of acceptance. Antonio would make declarations about solvency, adverse credit records, arrears and debt review. These are statements about his financial position, so he should check that every declaration accurately describes his circumstances. Supplying the information enables the bank to assess affordability, but it does not guarantee approval.

A loan for less than the requested amount can create a cash shortfall. Clause two point one point three permits the purchaser to reject an unacceptable lower loan by notifying the agent or seller. If a lower loan is accepted, or if the purchaser requested the lower amount, related clauses can treat the finance condition as fulfilled. They can also require an approved guarantee for the difference within seven working days. If the bank lends one million seven hundred thousand rand against a purchase price of one million nine hundred thousand rand, the buyers must find two hundred thousand rand for the price shortfall, with transaction costs still additional. This is an illustration of the clause, not a prediction of what the bank will offer.

Finally, withdrawal or breach can have consequences before the property is transferred. Clause six provides a period of seven working days to remedy a breach after written notice, followed by possible cancellation, a demand that the defaulting party perform the contract, and claims for damages. A demand for performance means a demand to carry out the agreed obligations rather than simply walk away. Clause five says the party responsible for cancellation can be liable for the estate agent’s commission. The recorded rate is six point three percent plus value added tax. On the stated price, that is one hundred and nineteen thousand seven hundred rand before that tax. This is a potential contractual claim, not a confirmed amount currently owed by Antonio. Whether a particular claim is enforceable depends on its circumstances and requires legal assessment. The absence of a deposit does not remove this potential exposure.

Why the document needs correction

The dates in the offer do not form one reliable timetable. Existing signatures are dated the seventeenth of February, two thousand and twenty-six. The offer is described as irrevocable until the twentieth of February. An irrevocable offer is an offer intended to remain open and not be withdrawn during the specified period. Occupation is recorded as starting on the first of March. The special condition then requires bond approval by the thirty-first of April, which is an impossible date because April has only thirty days, and says that the purchaser must leave by the first of May if approval is not obtained. These are all earlier than Antonio’s September correspondence.

Clause two point one separately requires approval within thirty days of both parties signing, and has the eighteenth of September, two thousand and twenty-six, handwritten beside it. The emails, meanwhile, require approval before the thirtieth of September. It would be unsafe to choose one of these dates as the controlling deadline without a written clarification agreed by the relevant parties. The conditional fourteen-day extension in the offer also should not be treated as an automatic extra period available to Antonio.

The special condition creates a further question about the continuing validity of the earlier agreement. It says that failing to obtain finance by its deadline makes the agreement automatically null and void. In ordinary terms, that wording says the agreement would cease to have effect if the specified financing condition were not met. Because the deadline is defective and the file appears to combine an older agreement with later additions, the supplied material does not establish whether there were valid extensions, a replacement agreement, or some other legally effective resolution. Clause nineteen requires amendments and an agreed cancellation to be in writing and signed. The missing history must therefore be established rather than assumed.

Antonio’s designated signature spaces appear blank, including his main purchaser signature and co-applicant acknowledgements. Nevertheless, the additional disclosure states that he has already initialled and signed the relevant pages. The document should accurately describe what has actually happened. Existing signatures and initials elsewhere are not proof that Antonio signed. The discrepancy does not establish his current legal position by itself, but it is a reason to obtain a clean and properly completed version.

The financing condition is broad. Clause two point one point one treats receipt of a bank’s written pre-agreement statement, quotation or offer as fulfilment of the condition. A pre-agreement statement sets out proposed credit terms before the lending agreement is completed. The offer does not set a maximum acceptable interest rate or a maximum affordable monthly repayment. This matters because obtaining some form of loan offer is not the same as confirming that its cost and conditions are acceptable to Antonio. The contemplated fourteen-day extension is at the discretion of the seller and listing agent, rather than guaranteed to him.

Arthur’s answer that commitment starts only on registration is therefore misleading. Registration is the stage at which ownership is transferred, but obligations under a signed and accepted offer can arise earlier, subject to its conditions. A financing condition can make completion dependent on obtaining finance without giving either party a general right to disregard all the other terms in the meantime. Even ooba’s published guidance explains that an accepted offer to purchase becomes legally binding. Antonio should not assume that he can sign now and withdraw without consequences at any point before registration. The exact effect of this particular document still needs legal assessment because of the conflicting dates, additions and incomplete signature information.

08 / 08

A practical sequence before committing

The recommended next step is for a South African conveyancing attorney to review a clean, current agreement before Antonio signs. The attorney should establish whether the February agreement is still valid, obtain any missing extensions or amendments, and confirm the authority of the seller’s executor and the attorney handling transfer. The final version should use consistent names and clearly record all current dates, the occupation arrangement, the finance condition and the signatures required. It should also resolve the difference between the general promise about working fixtures and the listed defective items.

The next part of that review is to confirm what Antonio will own and what he will pay. His ownership percentage should be stated, the proposed division of the price and costs should be understood, and his exposure to existing occupational rent or other historical charges should be addressed explicitly. A written estimate of transfer, bond registration and other transaction costs would make the upfront funding requirement concrete. These figures should be kept separate from the eventual monthly bank repayment and the seventeen-thousand-rand occupational rent.

Antonio and Denise should also have a separate written arrangement dealing with their relationship as co-owners. It should cover contributions, repairs, missed payments, what happens if either person dies, how sale proceeds are divided and how either person can exit or buy out the other. This can help clarify their rights against each other, but it does not automatically change the bank’s rights under the home loan.

Finally, the bank must separately confirm the actual amount offered, the repayment term, the interest rate, the monthly payment, any financed costs, the liability of each borrower and the conditions for releasing Antonio from the loan later. The proposed twenty-year term and possible inclusion of costs remain requests until the lender confirms them. With the legal document corrected, the ownership arrangement explicit and the lending terms available, Antonio can assess the commitment using defined obligations rather than the urgency of the accommodation deadline.
